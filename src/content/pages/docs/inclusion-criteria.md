App Fair publishes free, open-source apps for the public good, without built-in advertising or non-consensual tracking. Before a first submission, [open a discussion](https://github.com/orgs/appfair/discussions) describing the app, its users and who will maintain it. Acceptance requires App Fair review; passing CI is not acceptance.

## Prepare for review

- Keep the source and release history in a public repository. Include the actual license texts and source notices.
- Describe what the app does, which platforms it supports and how it uses data and permissions.
- Provide working support and privacy links and an accurate store listing.
- Maintain tests, release notes and a way to report defects.
- Confirm that you have the rights to distribute the code, assets and other included material.

> [!IMPORTANT] Keep the license notices
> The [App Fair template](https://github.com/appfair/day-appfair) includes AGPL-3.0-only with the App Fair Distribution Exception. Keep both license files and their notices when using those terms. Discuss different licensing with App Fair before submission; the template’s lint checks expect its license structure.

Read the [marketplace policies](/marketplace/) and the [catalog contribution requirements](https://github.com/appfair/appfair-apps/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md). App Fair and the destination stores review submissions independently.
